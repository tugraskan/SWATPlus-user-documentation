# 👽 1:1.2.1 Extraterrestrial Radiation

The solar constant, $$I_{SC}$$, describes solar energy incident on a surface normal to the Sun's rays at a mean Earth–Sun distance of one astronomical unit. The [SWAT 2009 theory](https://swat.tamu.edu/media/99192/swat2009-theory.pdf) uses the historical value

$$I_{SC} = 1367\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}} = 4.921\ \mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}\,h^{-1}}$$

On any given day, the extraterrestrial irradiance (rate of energy) on a surface normal to the rays of the sun, $$I_{0n}$$, is:

$$I_{0n} = I_{SC}E_0$$                                                                                                                                                           1:1.2.1                            &#x20;

where $$E_0$$ is the [Earth–Sun distance factor](../sun-earth-relationships/distance-between-earth-and-sun.md), and $$I_{0n}$$ has the same units as the solar constant. The irradiance on a horizontal surface is $$I_0$$:

$$I_0 = I_{0n} \cos\theta_z = I_{SC}E_0\cos\theta_z$$                                                                                                                       1:1.2.2                      &#x20;

where $$cos\theta_z$$, is defined in equation 1:1.1.3.

The amount of energy falling on a horizontal surface during a day is given by

$$H_0 = \int_{T_{SS}}^{T_{SR}} I_0\,dt = 2 \int_0^{T_{SR}} I_0\,dt$$                                                                                                                            1:1.2.3

Here $$H_0$$ is daily extraterrestrial irradiation in $$\mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}\,d^{-1}}$$. The limits use the [solar-time offsets](../sun-earth-relationships/solar-noon-sunrise-sunset-and-daylength.md): $$T_{SR}>0$$ for sunrise and $$T_{SS}<0$$ for sunset. Assuming $$E_0$$ is constant over the day and converting time to hour angle gives

$$H_0 = \frac{24}{\pi} I_{SC}E_0\int_0^{\omega T_{SR} }(\sin\delta \sin\phi+\cos\delta\cos\phi\cos\omega t)d\omega t$$                                                                    1:1.2.4

or

$$H_0 = \frac{24}{\pi} I_{SC}E_0[\omega T_{SR}\sin\delta\sin\phi+\cos\delta\cos\phi\sin(\omega T_{SR})]$$                                                                 1:1.2.5

Here $$I_{SC}$$ is the solar constant (4.921 $$MJ m^{-2} h^{-1}$$), $$\omega$$ is Earth's angular rotation rate (0.2618 rad/h), $$T_{SR}$$ is the sunrise offset in [equation 1:1.1.4](../sun-earth-relationships/solar-noon-sunrise-sunset-and-daylength.md), $$\delta$$ is solar declination, and $$\phi$$ is latitude. Multiplying the constants gives

$$H_0 = 37.59E_0[{\omega T_{SR} }\sin\delta \sin\phi+\cos\delta\cos\phi\sin(\omega T_{SR})]$$                                                                     1:1.2.6

### Source Code Implementation

| Equation | Relationship to current SWAT+ code |
| --- | --- |
| 1:1.2.1–1:1.2.2 | Theory relationships. [`cli_clgen.f90`](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/cli_clgen.f90#L68-L94) calculates the distance factor `dd` and solar-angle terms, but does not store separate `I0n` or `I0` values. |
| 1:1.2.3–1:1.2.5 | Theory derivation. The routine does not evaluate these intermediate integrals separately. |
| 1:1.2.6 | Transformed. The code calculates maximum possible radiation with coefficient 30 ([equation 1:1.2.7](1-1.2.2-solar-radiation-under-cloudless-skies.md)), then rescales it by 37.59/30 to obtain this quantity where Hargreaves PET needs it. |

The [Fortran conversion](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/et_pot.f90#L264) is:

```fortran
ramm = w%solradmx  * 37.59 / 30.
```

The same conversion also appears in [`climate_control.f90`](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/climate_control.f90#L183). `ramm` is calculated for PET; it is not a user input.

| Term or result | Source-code value | Origin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day of year | `time%day` | Simulation date; [day_start](../../../../introduction-1/simulation-settings/time.sim/day_start.md) sets the initial day |
| Latitude $$\phi$$ | `wgn_pms(iwgn)%latsin`, `%latcos` | [Weather generator latitude](../../../../introduction-1/climate/weather-wgn.cli/README.md), converted to trigonometric terms |
| Earth–Sun factor $$E_0$$ | `dd` | Calculated from day of year |
| Daily extraterrestrial radiation $$H_0$$ | `ramm`, where needed for Hargreaves PET | Calculated from `solradmx`; not a direct input |
| Solar constant and coefficients | `37.59`, `30.` | Built into the code; `I_SC` is not stored separately |

### Related literature

[Allen et al. (2006)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.agrformet.2006.05.012), p. 56, Eq. 1, give extraterrestrial radiation as $$R_a=G_{SC}\cos\theta/d^2$$, with solar constant $$G_{SC}=1367\ \mathrm{W\,m^{-2}}$$ and relative Earth–Sun distance $$d$$. For a horizontal surface, $$\theta$$ is the solar zenith angle, and because $$1/d^2=E_0$$, this is equations 1:1.2.1–1:1.2.2.
