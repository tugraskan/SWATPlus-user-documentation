# 1:1.2.2 Solar Radiation under Cloudless Skies

When solar radiation enters the earth's atmosphere, a portion of the energy is removed by scattering and absorption. The amount of energy lost depends on atmospheric transmittance, air composition, path length through the atmosphere, and radiation wavelength.

Due to the complexity of the process and the detail of the information required to accurately predict the amount of radiant energy lost while passing through the atmosphere, SWAT+ makes a broad assumption that roughly 20% of the extraterrestrial radiation is lost while passing through the atmosphere under cloudless skies. Using this assumption, the maximum possible solar radiation,  $$H_{MX}$$, at a particular location on the earth's surface is calculated as:

$$H_{MX} = 30.0E_0[{\omega T_{SR} }\sin\delta \sin\phi+\cos\delta\cos\phi\sin(\omega T_{SR})]$$                                                                   1:1.2.7

where the maximum possible solar radiation, $$H_{MX}$$, is the amount of radiation reaching the earth's surface under a clear sky  ($$MJ m^{-2} d^{-1}$$).

### Source Code Implementation

**Classification: Direct calculation with rounded constants and polar-day handling.** [`cli_clgen.f90`](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/cli_clgen.f90#L79-L94) evaluates equation 1:1.2.7 using `dd` for $$E_0$$, `h` for the bounded hour angle, and `ys`/`yc` for the latitude and declination terms:

```fortran
ys = wgn_pms(iwgn)%latsin * Sin(sd)
yc = wgn_pms(iwgn)%latcos * Cos(sd)
wst(iwst)%weat%solradmx = 30. * dd * (h * ys + yc * Sin(h))
```

The coefficient 30 is about 80% of the coefficient 37.59 in [equation 1:1.2.6](1-1.2.1-extraterrestrial-radiation.md). At high latitudes, the routine sets `h` to 0 or 3.1416 before this calculation. `solradmx` is a calculated maximum, not the measured or generated radiation for the day.

| Term or result | Source-code value | Origin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Latitude $$\phi$$ | `wgn_pms(iwgn)%latsin`, `%latcos` | [Weather generator latitude](../../../../introduction-1/climate/weather-wgn.cli/README.md) |
| Day of year and declination $$\delta$$ | `time%day`, `sd` | Simulation date; `sd` is calculated by [equation 1:1.1.2](../sun-earth-relationships/solar-declination.md) |
| Distance factor $$E_0$$ and hour angle | `dd`, `h` | Calculated by `cli_clgen.f90` |
| Clear-sky maximum $$H_{MX}$$ | `wst(iwst)%weat%solradmx` | Calculated result |
| Clear-sky coefficient | `30.` | Built-in constant; not a user input |

### Related literature

Allen (1996), *Journal of Irrigation and Drainage Engineering* 122(2), p. 97, Eq. 2, estimates clear-sky radiation as a fraction of extraterrestrial radiation that depends on elevation, $$K_T=0.75+2\times10^{-5}z$$, where $$z$$ is station elevation in meters. Equation 1:1.2.7 instead uses a fixed fraction of about 0.8 (30/37.59).
