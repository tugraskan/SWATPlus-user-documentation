# 1:1.2.5 Daily Net Radiation

Net radiation requires the determination of both incoming and reflected short-wave radiation and net long-wave or thermal radiation. Expressing net radiation in terms of the net short-wave and long-wave components gives:

$$H_{net}=H_{day}-\alpha H_{day}+H_L^{\downarrow}-H_L^{\uparrow}$$                                                                                            1:1.2.11

or

$$H_{net} = (1-\alpha) H_{day} + H_b$$                                                                                                                              1:1.2.12

Here $$H_{net}$$ is net radiation, $$H_{day}$$ is incoming short-wave radiation, $$\alpha$$ is albedo, and $$H_b=H_L^{\downarrow}-H_L^{\uparrow}$$ is net incoming long-wave radiation. Radiation quantities are in $$\mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}\,d^{-1}}$$. The arrows show the flux direction.

### 1:1.2.5.1 Net Short-Wave Radiation

Net short-wave radiation is defined as $$(1-\alpha) H_{day}$$. SWAT+ calculates a daily value for albedo as a function of the soil type, plant cover, and snow cover. When the snow water equivalent is greater than 0.5 mm,

$$\alpha=0.8$$                                                                                                                                                                   1:1.2.13      &#x20;

When snow water equivalent is at or below 0.5 mm and there is no leaf area in the HRU,

$$\alpha=\alpha_{soil}$$                                                                                                                                                                1:1.2.14

where $$\alpha_{soil}$$ is soil albedo. When leaf area is present and snow water equivalent is at or below 0.5 mm,

&#x20;$$\alpha=\alpha_{plant} (1-cov_{sol})+\alpha_{soil} cov_{sol}$$                                                                                                           1:1.2.15

where $$\alpha_{plant}$$ is the plant albedo (set at 0.23), and $$cov_{sol}$$ is the soil cover index. The soil cover index is calculated

$$cov_{sol}=\exp(-5.0\times10^{-5}CV)$$                                                                                                                 1:1.2.16

where $$CV$$ is aboveground biomass plus residue ($$kg  ha^{-1}$$). The code adds 0.1 to this mass before evaluating the exponential; see the source section below.

### 1:1.2.5.2 Net Long-Wave Radiation

Long-wave radiation is emitted from an object according to the radiation law:

$$H_R=\varepsilon \sigma T_K^{4}$$                                                                                                                                                          1:1.2.17

Here $$H_R$$ is radiant energy in $$\mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}\,d^{-1}}$$, $$\varepsilon$$ is emissivity, $$\sigma=4.903\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}\,K^{-4}\,d^{-1}}$$ is the Stefan–Boltzmann constant used in the earlier theory, and $$T_K$$ is mean air temperature in kelvin. The code uses `4.9e-9`. Net long-wave radiation uses a modified form of this relationship (Jensen et al., 1990):

&#x20;$$H_b=f_{cld} (\varepsilon_a -\varepsilon_{vs}) \sigma T_K^{4}$$                                                                                                                                 1:1.2.18

where $$H_b$$ is the net long-wave radiation ($$MJ m^{-2} d^{-1}$$), $$f_{cld}$$ is a factor to adjust for cloud cover, $$\varepsilon_a$$ is the atmospheric emittance, and $$\varepsilon_{vs}$$ is the vegetative or soil emittance.&#x20;

Wright and Jensen (1972) developed the following expression for the cloud cover adjustment factor, $$f_{cld}$$: &#x20;

$$f_{cld}=a \frac{H_{day}}{H_{MX}}-b$$                                                                                                                                                  1:1.2.19

where $$a$$ and $$b$$ are constants, $$H_{day}$$ is the solar radiation reaching the ground surface on a given day        ($$MJ m^{-2}d^{-1}$$), and $$H_{MX}$$ is the maximum possible solar radiation to reach the ground surface on a given day ($$MJ m^{-2}d^{-1}$$).&#x20;

The two emittances in equation 1:1.2.18 may be combined into a single term, the net emittance $$\varepsilon'$$. The net emittance is calculated using an equation developed by Brunt (1932):        &#x20;

$$\varepsilon'=\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_{vs}=-(a_1+b_1 \sqrt{(e)})$$                                                                                                                 1:1.2.20

where $$a_1$$ and $$b_1$$ are constants and $$e$$ is the vapor pressure on a given day ($$kPa$$). The calculation of $$e$$ is given in Chapter 1:2. Combining equations 1:1.2.18, 1:1.2.19, and 1:1.2.20 results in a general equation for net long-wave radiation: &#x20;

$$H_b=-[a \frac{H_{day}}{H_{MX}}-b] [a_1+b_1 \sqrt{(e)}] \sigma T_k^4$$                                                                                                        1:1.2.21

Experimental coefficient values from earlier literature are listed in Table 1:1-3. The current SWAT+ code uses the following fixed coefficients, proposed by Doorenbos and Pruitt (1977), in its PET radiation calculations:

$$H_b=-[0.9 \frac{H_{day}}{H_{MX}}+0.1] [0.34-0.139\sqrt{(e)}] \sigma T_k^4$$                                                                                     1:1.2.22

### Source Code Implementation

The source uses these relationships while calculating potential evapotranspiration (PET). The result depends on the PET method: Priestley–Taylor uses a fixed 0.23 albedo when snow is at or below 0.5 mm, whereas Penman–Monteith also calculates `rn` using the daily `albday` for maximum plant ET. Both use 0.8 when snow is above 0.5 mm. See [`et_pot.f90`](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/et_pot.f90#L115-L178). The daily albedo from equations 1:1.2.13–1:1.2.16, `albday`, is also used to estimate the [soil surface temperature](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/stmp_solt.f90#L104); with Priestley–Taylor, that is its only use.

| Equation | Relationship to current SWAT+ code |
| --- | --- |
| 1:1.2.11 | Conceptual radiation balance; the code evaluates its short-wave and net long-wave components rather than four separate fluxes. |
| 1:1.2.12 | Direct combination in `rn_pet = ralb + rout`; Penman–Monteith also uses `rn = ralb1 + rout` for a different albedo. |
| 1:1.2.13 | Direct: `albday = 0.8` when snow water equivalent is **greater than** 0.5 mm. |
| 1:1.2.14 | Direct: `albday` starts as top-layer soil albedo when snow is at or below 0.5 mm. |
| 1:1.2.15 | Direct when `pcom(j)%lai_sum > 0.`; the plant albedo is the built-in constant 0.23. |
| 1:1.2.16 | Modified: `eaj = exp(-5e-5 * (cover + 0.1))`, where `cover` is aboveground plant mass plus residue mass. |
| 1:1.2.17 | Physical Stefan–Boltzmann relationship; no standalone `H_R` result is calculated in this PET routine. |
| 1:1.2.18 | Implemented through `rbo`, `rto`, and `rout`, with a rounded Stefan–Boltzmann coefficient and a low-`solradmx` guard. |
| 1:1.2.19 | Direct with a guard: `rto = 0.9 * (solrad / solradmx) + 0.1`, or zero when `solradmx < 1.e-4`. |
| 1:1.2.20 | Direct: `rbo = -(0.34 - 0.139 * sqrt(ed))`, using calculated vapor pressure `ed`. |
| 1:1.2.21 | Transformed through the product `rbo * rto * 4.9e-9 * tk**4`. |
| 1:1.2.22 | Direct for the default coefficients, with `4.9e-9` in the code and the `solradmx` guard. The resulting long-wave term is added to method-specific short-wave radiation. |

The [daily albedo calculation](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/albedo.f90#L20-L34) is:

```fortran
cej = -5.e-5
cover = pl_mass(j)%ab_gr_com%m + pl_mass(j)%rsd_tot%m
eaj = Exp(cej * (cover + .1))
if (hru(j)%sno_mm <= .5) then
  albday = soil(j)%ly(1)%alb
  if (pcom(j)%lai_sum > 0.) albday = .23 * (1. - eaj) + soil(j)%ly(1)%alb * eaj
else
  albday = 0.8
end if
```

The [net long-wave and net-radiation statements](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/et_pot.f90#L160-L178) in the Penman–Monteith branch are:

```fortran
ralb1 = w%solrad * (1.0 - albday)
rbo = -(0.34 - 0.139 * Sqrt(ed))
if (w%solradmx  < 1.e-4) then
  rto = 0.
else
  rto = 0.9 * (w%solrad / w%solradmx) + 0.1
end if
rout = rbo * rto * 4.9e-9 * (tk**4)
rn = ralb1 + rout
rn_pet = ralb + rout
```

These are selected executable statements from the linked source, with intervening comments omitted. The code calculates `tk = w%tave + 273.15` and `ed = Ee(w%tave) * w%rhum` earlier in the routine. The historical coefficient table above does not represent user-selectable coefficients in this implementation.

**LTE HRU path.** The separate [`hru_lte_control.f90`](https://github.com/swat-model/swatplus/blob/6f515368525e7d84089ae0f9d33f4c9115de5679/src/hru_lte_control.f90#L162-L175) routine uses a simpler Priestley–Taylor radiation term, `ho = 23.9 * raobs * (1. - alb) / 58.3` with `alb = .23`. It does not use the standard HRU routine's `rbo`, `rto`, and `rout` long-wave calculation in that branch. This is a different source-code path from the equations above.

### Related literature

The [SWAT 2009 Theoretical Documentation](https://swat.tamu.edu/media/99192/swat2009-theory.pdf) gives equations 1:1.2.11–1:1.2.22 and the cited historical coefficients. Allen et al. (1989), *Agronomy Journal* 81, p. 660, Eq. 22, estimate daily net radiation with the same structure: $$(1-\alpha)R_s$$ minus a long-wave term containing $$(a_1-0.139\sqrt{e_d})(a\,R_s/R_{so}+b)$$, with $$\sigma=4.903\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}\,K^{-4}\,d^{-1}}$$. Their long-wave term averages the fourth powers of daily maximum and minimum air temperature, where SWAT+ uses mean air temperature, and their coefficients $$a_1$$, $$a$$, and $$b$$ vary with day of year and cloudiness.
